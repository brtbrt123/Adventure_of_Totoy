# Chapter 1 creatures (reference art, v1)

Status: **for the human's review only.** Nothing here is imported into `art/src` or wired into the game, and nothing is committed. HP, damage, ranges, telegraph lengths and state durations are core decisions. The tick numbers below are suggestions only.

Generated by `python tools/art/make_chapter1_creatures_art.py` (Pillow only, fixed seeds). Two runs give byte-identical files. The script includes a `--debug <mossling|boar|moth|stag> <out.png>` mode that renders one creature's frames at 6x.

Palette: Totoy 36 with hard alpha. Every atlas is checked with `artlib.check_png` plus a reserved-colour scan. **No gold (`x`,`y`) or red (`z`,`A`) appears anywhere**, so the gold/red telegraphs, parry diamonds and warning rings stay presentation's job. Colour language:
- moss and leaves: grass ramp
- hide and bark: dirt ramp
- antlers, tusks and spine tips: stone ramp and cream
- Aspect fragments and spirit light: teal and water_5/6
- **lilac `v`**: Aspect corruption (the boar's eye, the moth's body and wing spots, the stag's eye, shoulder veins and the motes that leave it on defeat)

Construction: every creature is a rig of shaded primitives. It gets a 5-step ramp with up-left light, dark contours where parts overlap, and a 1 px ink outline like the Colossus. Moss uses a hashed texture that moves with the body. Right-facing only: mirror around the anchor for left.

The JSON files hold, per frame, the rect and the anchor (`x,y,w,h,ax,ay`, the same keys as `sprites.json`). Per state they hold the frame count, `ticks_per_frame` (60/s; `"telegraph"` = choose the frame from telegraph progress), loop and a note. Suggested in-game names: `creature_<id>_<state>_<n>` (n from 1), `fx_ch1_*_<n>` and `ui_*`.

## UI (`ui_ch1_v1`)

- `ui_enemy_bar_frame_1` (32x8, anchor (16,8)): an ink border with a stone bevel and teal end caps, echoing the boss frame. The HP track sits at x3-28 y2-3 and the posture track at x6-25 y5.
- Fills (32x8 cells aligned to the frame; crop them to width at runtime): `ui_enemy_bar_hp_1` (grass_6 with a cream top line, like the boss HP), `_hp_ghost_1` (stone_6), `_posture_1` (lilac), `_posture_broken_1` (water_6). These match `hud.gd`'s boss colours.
- `ui_aggro_mark_small_1` (8x8) and `ui_aggro_mark_1..4` (16x16, anchor bottom-centre): a pale '!' with an ink outline. The four frames are pop, overshoot, settle and glint.

## Known limits / not judged

- **Not judged: whether these look charming or read well in motion at 320x180.** That is the human's call. I checked still sheets zoomed and a few GIF frames only.
- The stag's antlers are busy at 1x: two crowns, vine wraps and tufts. The silhouette reads, but individual tines blur together. The thorn vines are subtle.
- The glowmoth uses a three-quarter front view, not a strict side view (see above).
- Lunge, charge and slam frames are drawn near the anchor. Real travel distance is the core's job.
- The hurt "flash" frames lighten every colour by one ramp step. If presentation already flashes with a shader, drop frame 1's lightening.
- The stag's roots in `phase2_roar` are part of the sprite (decorative). Damaging spikes should be separate `fx_ch1_root_spike` instances placed by the core, with presentation drawing the red warning first.
- The mossling's spawn mound and death pile, plus the boar's mound, are short-lived. Remove the entity after the last frame.
- GIF spacing and timing are illustrative only.
